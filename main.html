My Experience Playing the Drums

How I started

Playing the drums is a test in multitasking and a test of the relationship with anyone you are living with. I started playing the drums about 4 years ago at this point in my parents’ basement. I started because one day a couple of my friends, who play guitar, wanted to start a band but didn’t know anyone who played the drums. I’m still not sure why they decided to get someone who had no idea how to play drums to be the drummer, especially because drums are not something you can just pick up like that. Unfortunately, we ended up at different colleges by the time I was good enough to actually play them but that’s how I ended up getting started.

Some of the fundamentals

If you want to learn the drums there are a few things you need to learn first. The first thing is how to hold the sticks. Believe me it is not fun taking lessons a year into playing only to find out that you’ve been holding the sticks wrong in a way that holds you back from playing faster and more controlled. The next thing you need to learn is limb independence. Your hands and feet do not work together very well so consistently doing limb independence practice is one of the most important things for making learning new things easier. The next is how to play on time. The drummer controls the song, and everyone follows them so if you aren’t playing evenly the rest of the band will fall apart and the songs will sound bad. The next thing to learn is the rudiments. Rudiments are boring and they suck to play because they are just mindless repetitive exercises however, they are the basis of pretty much everything you do. Also playing things slowly will help you. Playing garbage fast is still garbage. Then finally play songs you actually like. The drums weren’t my first instrument my first instrument was the trombone, and I hated it because in the music class I was in, I didn’t get to play any of the songs I liked. This goes for any instrument, but you aren’t going to continue unless you like the music, you’re playing.

What its like to play them

Drums are powerful and you can feel them through your entire body when you play and no matter how softly you play them they are going to be loud. Most drummers I know love that loud energy when they’re playing and I’m no exception. You feel like a machine that turns practice into noise complaints. Then playing them will make you appreciate rhythm far more. Once you understand the art of creating rhythm it makes music sound better. Its hard to explain but it really does.

The difficult parts of the drums

Like I said before drums are LOUD. If you play them, you basically have to accept that people around you are going to hate that you play them. No one like not being able to hear themselves think. This is why there are so few drummers because if you live with anyone or in a somewhat densely populated area, you’re going to disturb people. I recommend finding a storage unit in the middle of nowhere to play them in, like I do. The other thing that sways people away is the fact that they are very expensive. Basically, the bare minimum you’ll have to spend to get started with a full drum kit is about $300-$500, depending on whether you want a new kit or one you find on Facebook Marketplace. Then, finally, the drums are just very daunting. It took me 3 years to be mediocre at them. It’s going to take an incredible amount of practice to even start to be able to play songs, but that’s how it is with any instrument. But I still love playing the drums, and I think that everything I just mentioned is worth it for such an amazing instrument.
